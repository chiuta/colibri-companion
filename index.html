A map of open models for pictures, video, voice and music, and a box to make pictures with a server on your own network.

Automations

Chain steps: read a page or a folder, ask the model, save or notify. Anything that leaves your computer needs your approval.

Memory and constellation

Long chats stay coherent through automatic summaries. Pin notes, find related chats and see how your conversations connect on a map.

Accessibility for everyone

Seven ready presets, a colour-blind palette, text up to 300%, a highly legible font, read aloud, reduced motion, simple menus and Easy Read answers.

Know your models

Fact sheets and a comparison of local and online AIs, so you choose by what you need.

Folders and documents

Model duels

Ask two anonymous models the same question, vote for the better answer, then see who was who. A leaderboard (Elo) builds up on your computer, with CSV, Markdown and JSONL export.

Branch a conversation

Go back to any earlier message and try another direction in a copy. The original stays untouched.

Test your automations

Run an automation on sample inputs several times and see how often it gets it right, with an honest margin of error. Nothing is saved or sent during a test.

Proof, not promises

A journal of every request that reaches the internet, with host names only, next to the list of open connections. Check for yourself.

Chat with your documents

Add files and ask questions. The app finds the relevant passages on your computer (keyword search, no cloud) and shows which ones it used under each answer.

MCP tools, with your approval

Connect MCP servers — programs you start yourself. The model can ask to use a tool; every single call needs your OK, and results are treated as data.

Knows your computer

It finds your graphics cards by itself and tells you in plain words if the model fits in memory, why it is slow (swap, other programs, an idle card) and what to do. It only advises: it never changes a setting on its own.

Every model on your disk

Models that colibri did not download (a folder copied by hand, Gemma, anything with a config.json and weights) appear in My models as “Local copy”. Add a folder, or drag it onto the window, and the .gguf files inside are listed: Convert and use does the rest. The app never deletes a model it did not download.

Your chat history, portable

Save every conversation as one JSON file in an open, documented format (or one Markdown file per chat) and import it back, here or in CartaColibri. A newer copy of a conversation replaces an older one, never the reverse, and a damaged or hostile file cannot harm your history.

What it does today, and what is still only code

Checked against the code of this version. Nothing has been run against a real colibri model, a real ROUTE_TRACE recording or real GPU hardware.

In the window now

Will it be fast? An estimate before you download

Under each model in the Models window you see a speed range such as “≈ 2.5–125 tokens/s”. It comes from your memory size, your drive speed and the catalogue's figures: a wide range, never a measurement. The drive test (about 4 seconds) narrows it.

Record the expert routing (ROUTE_TRACE)

The app starts the model with ROUTE_TRACE on, then analyses the recording on your computer and tells you whether pinning the most-used experts, a smarter cache or next-layer prefetching would save drive reads. These are cost-model replays, not speed measurements; under 200 tokens the numbers are only indicative.

Auto-tune for this computer

Tries pinned memory (PIN_GB) and compute threads one at a time, in at most 8 runs, measures real tokens per second, keeps the fastest and remembers it for this computer and model. The conversation length is never changed. It restarts the model several times and you can cancel. A switch also adapts the expert cache to the conversation.

Light models, including agents

A catalogue of 11 small models with the RAM tier each needs (4, 8, 16 or 32 GB). In the Models window, “Light models” shows the ones that fit your memory, an “agents only” filter (documented tool calling), a button that copies the “ollama run” command, and the source page for each. Checked against public pages on 2026-11-17; none of these models was run.

Citation check

Ported from TEZAUR. In the Documents window, paste a text whose sentences cite your files, like [@report, p. 3], and see for each citation whether the cited passage backs the sentence. It compares words and figures, not meaning: “supported” points to where to look and is not proof. It runs on this computer.

Reading scans with LegatorOCR

If the separate LegatorOCR program is installed, the Documents window can run it on a scan and add the text to your documents, so the model can answer from the scan. Tested against a real LegatorOCR on one generated page of clean text, not on a real book scan. OCR text is not a verified transcription.

Usage for each answer, beside the text

While an answer is being written, a panel beside it shows the time, the first token, tokens in and out, speed now, average and peak with a small graph, the share of the context used, and the processor, memory, swap and graphics card of this computer. Afterwards the “Usage” button under each answer reopens it. Figures marked ≈ are estimated from the pieces received; the machine's load is read only for a server on this computer. Tested without a window and with a stand-in engine, not against a real model.

Scholar tools: the rest

Also ported from TEZAUR: the Bibliography section of the Documents window converts BibTeX, RIS and CSL JSON and copies a reference list (APA, Harvard, IEEE); tested on generated entries only. Still a library only: passage retrieval with page numbers for cited answers, a provenance receipt and an AI-use declaration in 8 languages.

Search by meaning, with a server you start

In Documents, Research tools, Search by meaning: give the address of an embeddings server (for example corbul serve --encoder), prepare the shelf and tick the option; the keyword and meaning rankings are merged. If the server fails, the search falls back to keywords. Tested only against a stand-in server and made-up vectors, not with a real model.

Your own models and a portable history

Models: My models also lists folders that colibri did not download, and Load models from a folder finds .gguf files and converts one with colibri. History: export and import all conversations as JSON (open format) or Markdown. Tested only with simulated files and a stand-in engine: no real GGUF conversion or real model start has been tried.

Screenshots in this section's videos and in the tutorial are captured from the real window, running against a stand-in engine with example models.

corbul: an engine written from scratch, still an early alpha

Separate from the app: a command-line program and local server written in Rust that runs models without colibri. It has only been tested on tiny synthetic models and tokenizer reference files. No real downloaded model, no graphics card, and no Windows, macOS or ARM computer has run it yet.

Reads real model files

GGUF and safetensors (also split into several files), 14 quantisation types, a tokenizer for 12 model families that matches llama.cpp's reference files in all 46 test cases, and a downloader for Hugging Face. i-quants and BERT-style models are not supported.

Ordinary and giant models

Chat models and mixture-of-experts models run on the processor; models larger than your memory are read from disk (slow, limited by the disk) or shared across computers on your network (authenticated, not encrypted). DeepSeek-style models, images and sound are not available in corbul yet.

A local server with its own web page

Speaks the OpenAI, Ollama and Anthropic styles, with tool calling and structured output (JSON, schema, regular expression, grammar). Embedding endpoints work; reranking is not implemented.

Checks you can inspect

Quotes marked « » must be copied word for word from your sources, a generation can be sealed in a receipt and replayed, text can be watermarked, and a planner tells you before loading whether a model will fit. Also Romanian text tools, search, quantisation, merging, steering and local retrieval.

Speeds are estimates; nothing was benchmarked. The command list and every limit are in docs/engine (ENGINE.md, QUICKSTART.md, INNOVATIONS.md, CHANGES-SECURITY.md); run corbul capabilities for the limits of the program you have. Translations of those pages are still being prepared.

First time opening it?

Windows

Windows may show a blue “SmartScreen” notice because the app is new and not yet signed. Click “More info”, then “Run anyway”.

macOS

macOS may say it cannot check the app. Right-click it, choose “Open”, then “Open” again. You only need to do this once.

Linux

Extract the archive and run ./colibri-companion. To add it to your application menu, run packaging/linux/install.sh from the extracted folder.

Just curious? Try it without a model

Start it from a terminal with --demo to explore the whole interface with a pretend model.

colibri-companion --demo

Prefer to build it yourself?

You need Rust. Then:

git clone https://github.com/chiuta/colibri-companion
cd colibri-companion
cargo run --release -p colibri-companion

Questions, answered

What is colibri, and what does this app add?

colibri is an engine that runs AI language models on your own computer. It is controlled from the command line. Colibri Companion gives it a window: it guides you, downloads the model and offers a normal chat.

Is it really private?

Your conversations never leave your computer: the model runs locally and chats are saved in a folder on it. The app uses the internet only for what you ask: fetching the colibri engine if it is missing, downloading the models you pick, a daily check for a newer app version on GitHub (you can turn it off), and the platforms you connect. The Resources window lists every such request, host names only.

What kind of computer do I need?

The app tells you. It measures your memory and free disk space and shows only the models that fit. More memory means bigger, smarter models. Python 3 is needed by colibri; if it is missing, the app shows you what to do.

Does it include colibri and the models?

No. The app is small on purpose. It helps you get colibri (and tells you if it is missing) and downloads the model you pick from its official source. Each model has its own licence, which the app shows you before downloading.

Where are my files, and how do I remove everything?

Settings and conversations live in one data folder, and the models in the models folder you chose (by default “colibri-models” in your home folder). Delete those folders and the app itself, and nothing else remains.

Which languages does it speak?

English, Română, Français, Italiano, Español, Português, Deutsch and Magyar. It picks your system language at first start; change it any time in Settings. The translations were made with AI help and have not yet been checked by native speakers — corrections are very welcome.

Something is wrong. Where do I get help?

The app explains problems in plain words and shows what to do next. In Settings you can open the log. If you are still stuck, open an issue on GitHub and attach the log — we will gladly help.

Does it ever go online?

Only for platforms you connect and use yourself. Pages and repositories are read only when you ask. Tokens are kept on your computer only if you tick “remember”, in a file readable by you alone, not encrypted. Every request is logged, host names only.

Is there a guide for beginners?

Yes. A short tour opens the first time you start the app, and you can reopen it from the More menu. The model list has a search box and quick filters (ready now, recommended, fast, code, creative, math, multilingual).

Can it read my documents and use tools?

Yes. Document chat searches your files locally (keyword-based, not meaning-based) and cites the passages. MCP tools work only with servers you add and start; each call asks for your approval. Early feature: tool use depends on how well the model follows the format, and it has not yet been tested with many real models. Each document can also be listed as a citable source and the list exported as BibTeX, RIS or CSL JSON.

A window is bigger than my screen. How do I close it?

Press Esc: it closes the open window, even from a text box. Since alpha.7 every window stays inside the screen and its content scrolls, so the title bar with the close button is always reachable.
